Color and eyedropper

The Color panel sets the color your brushes paint with. You can keep two colors ready to swap between, jump straight to black or white, and pick up any color that is already in your drawing.

What you'll learn

  • Choose a color on the color wheel.
  • Use the foreground, background, black and white swatches.
  • Touch and hold the canvas to pick a color from your drawing.

Choose a color

In the Color panel, drag around the outer ring to choose a hue, then drag inside the ring to make that hue lighter, darker, brighter or more muted. The wheel is based on how people see color, so steps that look even on the wheel also look even in your painting. If you prefer a square or a triangle inside the ring, you can switch its shape.

To type an exact value, such as a hex code, select the pencil button in the corner of the panel or double-click the color swatch. A short test stroke next to your drawing helps too, because a color can look different on white paper than it does next to other paint.

Keep two colors ready

The two large circles at the bottom of the panel are the foreground and background colors. Brushes paint with the foreground color, and the arrow button swaps the two, which is handy when you are going back and forth between a light and a dark. Gradient and some shapes use both colors.

The small black and white circles let you paint with pure black or white straight away, without losing the two colors you’ve chosen. The checkered circle is transparent paint: while it is selected, your brush removes paint instead of adding it. Select the foreground color again when you want to paint normally.

Pick a color from the drawing

The quickest way to pick up a color from your drawing is to touch and hold the canvas with one finger. After a moment, a magnifying loupe appears just above your finger, so you can see exactly which color is under the crosshair. Slide to the color you want and lift your finger to pick it. While you are holding, tap a second finger to switch between the color you see and the color on the selected layer alone.

With a mouse or pen, press I, or select the color picker button at the edge of the screen in Sketch. The same loupe follows the pointer. Click with the mouse, or touch the canvas with the pen and lift it, to pick the color, and you go straight back to the brush you were using. Press Escape or tap with a finger if you change your mind.

In Paint and Photo you can also choose the Eyedropper tool from the toolbar. Its settings choose whether it picks the color you see or only the selected layer’s color, and a larger Sample size averages out texture or noise.

To keep colors for later, add them to a palette in Palettes.
