## Arrange panels and toolbars

Keep the brush list and Layers panel visible while following the tutorial; these are the controls you will return to most often. Move panels and toolbars to a position that leaves the drawing visible under your pen hand. Open their settings to change which controls they contain rather than keeping every panel open.

Tool settings apply to the active tool. When you switch from a brush to a selection tool, check the settings again: a brush's opacity and a selection's edge settings affect different operations. The [brush engine reference](/docs/advanced/brush-engine/) covers the detailed brush controls.

## Navigate without changing the drawing

Rotate the canvas view to put a long curve at a comfortable angle for your hand, then return the view to its original orientation. Panning, zooming, rotating, and mirroring the view leave the artwork unchanged. Use [Transformations](/docs/tools/transforms/) when you want to move or reshape the actual drawing.

Fit the canvas to the window when you need to see the whole document. Use actual-size viewing when checking fine marks or the edge of a fill; a zoomed-out view can hide small gaps.

## Use Zen mode

Enter Zen mode after choosing a brush and layer to hide the surrounding controls while drawing. Leave it when you need to inspect the layer stack or change settings; hiding a panel does not change the active tool or layer.

If you often draw this way, keep undo and canvas navigation accessible through your keyboard or pen buttons. Set those up in [Input settings](/docs/advanced/input/) before hiding the controls.
