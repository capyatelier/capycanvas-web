This stage produces the opened photo, cropped to a 5:4 frame.

## 1. Open the photo

Choose **File > Open…**, or press **Ctrl+O**, and select the photo
([Opening and saving](/docs/files/open-save/)). The photo opens in a drawing
tab of its own.

![The Layers panel with the terrarium photo layer above a hidden Paper layer.](shot:photo/open-layers)

The drawing has one layer, named after the file, above a hidden **Paper**
layer. In the example, the layer is named "terrarium".

## 2. Choose a ratio

Choose **Edit > Image > Crop**, select **Crop** in the Tools toolbar, or press
**C** ([Crop](/docs/transform/crop/)). A frame with handles appears over the
photo, with the crop bar along the bottom of the canvas.

Select **Ratio** in the crop bar and choose **4:5**. The frame becomes the
largest frame of that ratio, centered on the canvas. Its orientation follows
the photo, so a landscape photo gets a 5:4 frame.

![The crop bar with the Ratio menu open.](shot:photo/crop-ratio)

## 3. Position the frame

If a horizon or another level line in your photo is tilted, straighten the
photo before you move the frame. Select **Straighten** in the crop bar and
draw a line along the tilted line. The crop turns to match, and the frame
returns to the center.

Drag inside the frame with a pen or a mouse to move it. The example moves the
frame to the right, to give the rock and the tips of the fern even margins.

![The crop frame moved to the right over the terrarium photo, with the thirds overlay and the crop bar.](shot:photo/crop-bar)

## 4. Apply the crop

Select **Apply** in the crop bar, or press **Enter**. The canvas takes the size
of the frame: 2000 × 1600 pixels in the example. The cropped pixels stay on
the layer, hidden, unless **Delete Cropped** is on in the crop bar.
