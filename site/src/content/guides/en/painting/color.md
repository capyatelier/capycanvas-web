## Choose a color

In the **Color** panel, drag around the outer ring to choose a hue, then drag inside the ring to make that hue lighter, darker, brighter or more muted. The wheel is based on how people see color, so steps that look even on the wheel also look even in your painting. If you prefer a square or a triangle inside the ring, you can switch its shape.

To type an exact value, such as a hex code, select the pencil button in the corner of the panel or double-click the color swatch. A short test stroke next to your drawing helps too, because a color can look different on white paper than it does next to other paint.

## Keep two colors ready

The two large circles at the bottom of the panel are the **foreground** and **background** colors. Brushes paint with the foreground color, and the arrow button swaps the two, which is handy when you are going back and forth between a light and a dark. **Gradient** and some shapes use both colors.

The small black and white circles let you paint with pure black or white straight away, without losing the two colors you've chosen. The checkered circle is **transparent paint**: while it is selected, your brush removes paint instead of adding it. Select the foreground color again when you want to paint normally.

## Pick a color from the drawing

Choose the **Eyedropper** tool, or press **I**, then click the part of the drawing whose color you want. By default it picks the color you see on screen, with all layers combined. You can switch it to read only the selected layer, and choose a larger sample size to average out texture or noise. On a touch screen, you can also touch and hold the canvas to pick a color.

To keep colors for later, add them to a palette in [Palettes](/docs/painting/palettes/).
